Monday's Entry 07-27-2026

Life within life. That is the phrase that kept turning over in my head the last few days after noticing an interesting development in the soil of our monstera house plant. Let’s dive into it.

When I married my wife, she had a small monstera, given to her by a friend moving out of their apartment who had little luck at keeping it alive, and was working diligently on returning it to proper health. Not only did she get the plant back on track, she was helping it thrive! We used to joke that the plant was a reflection of her true self - when it was growing a new leaf, my wife was growing as well, when its leaves were drooping and in need of something specific, so was she. I am not quite sure if this could be qualified in any way other than the fact that it was represented in the movie “E.T. The Extra Terrestrial” between the alien and a sunflower, but it became a fun truth shared between the two of us. And now, since we brought them up with that last reference - on to aliens.

A few days ago, I went to water our monstera (which now lives in a much larger pot than several years ago, with thirteen leaves and two new ones on the way) but stopped myself in sudden amazement. There was a plump, yellow stalk protruding from the soil. It was rounded at the top with knob-like ridges at the base. I took a step back and asked my wife, with mustered courage, “um…what is that?” We both stared at what looked like an alien finger for a good few seconds, then stared at each other and began to quietly freak out.

A quick internet search calmed us down. Leucocoprinus birnbaumii is what the organism went by, formally, or ‘flowerpot parasol’ if you’re on a first name basis. Apparently it was a sign of the burgeoning mycelium fungal network within the monstera pot, brought to the surface in the form of a flowering mushroom due to moist soil conditions most likely. (I made a mental note to push the watering out to three or four weeks instead of every two.) We were reassured that this would not harm the plant and that it was only toxic if ingested, so we decided to leave it in order to watch the life cycle.

The next morning, the rounded top was fully open - a parasol! Ah! Wonderfully named. Underneath were rows of tiny gills and from these flowed many, many microscopic spores into the surrounding soil.

Later that day, the flower was curled up, hunched over, and lying on the soil. Just as quickly as it came to the surface, it served its purpose and began its process of disappearance. The spores were delivered and I’m sure we may see more of these strange, alien, yellow parasols - life within life.

For years we watched as our little monstera grew larger notched leaves, and stretched its stalks upward and outward, and flung its aerial roots in every direction. For years my wife meticulously researched proper soil conditions and potting techniques. Our life carried on, day after day, and so did this plant’s. What we didn’t realize, however, was that there was even more life growing under the surface.

The fungal mycelium network is such a bizarre and amazing phenomenon. It is branched in more than a hundred different ways throughout the soil and offers a highway for exchange of returned nutrients from organic matter breakdown for sugars produced by the plant. An elaborate framework, completely hidden from plain site. All this time, while we admired the growth of new leaves, slowly unfurling in shades of bright green, we didn’t even know about all of the growth happening deep within the pot. Within the life we have come to monitor and admire, was yet another life that wasn’t asking for any admiration.

Or, maybe, it was. Maybe that little yellow umbrella that shocked and then delighted us for a span of less than 48 hours was the fungus’ way of saying, “hello world! I am here! And I matter!” And maybe…that isn’t the case. Maybe it was just the next step in its mission to continue growing.

It’s hard to tell which possibility is the truth, but for now I will tip my hat and nod to the impressive interwoven highway of mycelium - thank you for all that you’ve done to help our little monstera (which is now, admittedly, our big monstera).

How clever and hidden you have been, after all this time. Remind me never to play hide and seek with fungus.

Thank you for visiting Toad Press, here’s to a great week with good human vibes for all of us.

- Tariq

Saturday's Entry 07-25-2026

Free samples at grocery stores are a thing of wonder. There is something about driving (or walking, or biking if you live in a walkable city) to a store for the purpose of transacting money for goods, only to be met by happy store workers asking you to try their products - for free! I’ve always loved the idea of free samples and they always brighten a shopping trip.

Today, we stopped at a newly opened grocery store with the honest intention of finding a nice pastry to snack on. We were initially greeted by the smells of papaya and oranges. We also saw some tasty lychee fruit for sale. In all honesty, the fruit looked delicious, but our mission was for a pastry.

I should also note that I hadn’t had my morning coffee at this point (normal routine fell victim to a Saturday morning change-up and a desire to ‘get the shopping done early’) so I was also hoping for a hot cup. Soon, we found a small cafe in the middle of the shop.

Hooray!

We ordered a 12 oz cup of coffee ($1.99, not a bad price in today’s economy) and marveled at huge, warm chocolate chip cookies. These were $3.99, but our hunger and joy at finding what we embarked to find made it easy to decide to purchase one. The options were chocolate chip or chocolate chip + walnut. I’m a sucker for walnuts in my sweets. I’m also a sucker for anything that follows a “+.” New game + after finishing a video game? Let’s go!

Fresh was an understatement. The cookie was hot and the chocolate melted as we broke the large, rounded cookie in half for each of us to share. It was a perfect snack to pair with a good cup of coffee. My wife saved the big chunks of walnuts for me.

Even more perfect? The free samples around the corner. I still had cookie crumbling out of my mouth when I saw, with great excitement, a stand manned by a single employee handing out a decent-sized sample of pasta alfredo paired with a small piece of garlic bread. Two for one! Like a kid eyeing a house with king size candy bars on Halloween while a fun size candy is still melting in his mouth, I darted towards it.

After this, we found a stand serving a spinach pie coupled with mediterranean rice and veggie medley and a perfectly crisp pita chip. Next, 12 month aged gouda cheese. And, finally, pitted pimento olives, two per sample. All of this, plus half a chocolate chip (+ walnut) cookie and a cup of hot coffee, filled us right up. The delight was both spoken and unspoken. A dimpled grin.

Now, we did not purchase any of the items we sampled and, to be truthful, we rarely do. Once, at Costco Wholesale, a customer held a strange stance at a sample counter and exclaimed, “I’m not one of those people who samples and doesn’t buy! I always buy what I sample!” as if to prove a point. Maybe this stemmed from some self consciousness that I am sure we all encounter when taking a free sample. I resolve that with a “thank you” but I suppose a declaration of firm commitment to buying the product is yet another resolution.

Regardless, sometimes we do buy a sampled product, and we usually continue to buy it time and time again afterwards. I think therein lies the beauty of free samples. They may not always hit it out of the park but, every now and then, for different people, different products will resonate concordantly with their own personal palate.

The other half of the beauty of free samples (if it ultimately only has two halves) is the joy of discovery. I feel that we humans love the adventure of searching and finding, especially when not expected. It is this process that can turn a simple grocery store trip into a treasure hunt. And who doesn’t love a good treasure hunt?

We’ve all got a bit of a pirate inside us, I’m sure!

Thank you for reading Toad Press today, and good luck on finding your own trove of free samples soon.

- Tariq
